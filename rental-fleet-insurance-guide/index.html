01 / Describe the risk

Write the operation before you request terms.

The first question is not simply how many cars you own. An underwriter needs to understand how the vehicles generate revenue, who may drive them, where they travel, and which controls apply before and after every rental.

Use this one-paragraph template

[Business name] rents [number and type of vehicles] in [primary state and territory]. Rentals are [daily, weekly, or monthly] and booked [directly, through platforms, or both]. Vehicles are garaged at [location]. We [allow or prohibit] delivery, gig, or other commercial use. Renters are screened by [actual process]. Coverage should begin on [date].

Disclose edge cases early. Delivery use, long-term rentals, out-of-state travel, young drivers, specialty vehicles, and mixed personal or business use can materially change carrier appetite and terms.

02 / Build the file

The rental fleet insurance checklist.

Send what you have, but label what is missing. A smaller, reconciled file is more useful than a large folder with conflicting schedules and unexplained documents.

01

Business and operations

  • Legal business name, entity type, EIN, operating address, and years in business
  • Coverage start date, states of operation, garaging locations, and service territory
  • Direct, platform, or mixed rental model; typical rental length; and vehicle delivery process
  • Rental agreement, prohibited uses, inspection process, and incident-reporting procedure
02

Vehicles and ownership

  • Complete schedule with VIN, year, make, model, mileage, and current value
  • Titleholder, financing or lease information, purchase date, and garaging address
  • Maintenance, recall, key-control, GPS, theft-prevention, and vehicle-recovery practices
  • Planned purchases, replacement vehicles, and any specialty, vintage, luxury, or modified units
03

Renters, drivers, and use

  • Minimum renter requirements and the process used to verify licenses and driving history
  • Rules for additional drivers, employee drivers, out-of-state travel, and vehicle handoff
  • Whether delivery, rideshare, gig, courier, commercial hauling, or other business use is allowed
  • Booking, payment, deposit, telematics, and post-rental inspection controls
04

Current insurance and losses

  • Current policy and declaration pages, including limits, deductibles, and effective dates
  • Carrier-issued loss runs for the available policy history, plus open-claim status
  • A short explanation of larger losses and what changed afterward
  • Prior cancellations, nonrenewals, declinations, coverage gaps, and requested policy changes

03 / Ask better questions

Compare how the policy works, not premium alone.

Names and availability vary by state and carrier. Use these questions with the licensed professional handling your account rather than assuming a coverage applies.

01

Liability while a vehicle is rented

Who qualifies as an insured, which drivers and uses are eligible, and what limits, exclusions, or state-specific requirements apply?

02

Damage to fleet-owned vehicles

Which causes of loss are covered, how is a vehicle valued, what deductibles apply, and how do renter damage agreements interact with the policy?

03

State-required auto coverages

Which uninsured or underinsured motorist, no-fault, personal injury protection, or other coverages are required or available where the fleet operates?

04

Risks outside the auto policy

Does the business also need general liability, workers compensation, cyber, umbrella, property, or another policy for exposures that commercial auto does not address?

04 / Prevent delays

Five reasons a submission stalls.

Most avoidable back-and-forth begins with an inconsistency, not the absence of a polished document.

  1. 01

    The application and rental agreement tell different stories

    Reconcile who may drive, where vehicles may go, rental duration, prohibited uses, and incident procedures before submission.

  2. 02

    The vehicle schedule is incomplete or stale

    Match every VIN to the titleholder, garaging location, current value, financing status, and requested coverage.

  3. 03

    Renter screening is described only as 'we check licenses'

    Document the actual verification steps, decision rules, exceptions, and records retained for every rental.

  4. 04

    Loss runs arrive without context

    Explain what happened, current claim status, amount paid or reserved when available, and the control added afterward.

  5. 05

    The effective date leaves no room for underwriting

    Start early enough to gather carrier questions, compare material terms, and correct missing information before the current policy ends.

05 / Work backward

A practical renewal timeline.

Starting a new fleet? Test insurability and indicative cost before committing to the full vehicle plan.

  1. 90 to 60 days

    Define the operation

    Confirm the rental model, territory, vehicle plans, screening rules, desired effective date, and material changes. Request loss runs and current policy documents.

  2. 60 to 30 days

    Reconcile the file

    Match vehicles, titles, values, drivers, rental agreements, and loss history. Ask the licensed professional handling the account to identify gaps before approaching markets.

  3. 30 days to effective date

    Answer and compare

    Respond to underwriting questions promptly. Compare exclusions, eligible use, deductibles, limits, payment terms, and servicing expectations, not premium alone.

06 / FAQ

Questions independent operators ask.

Can I use a personal auto policy for vehicles I rent to customers?

Do not assume that you can. Personal auto policies often do not cover vehicles used for work, and renting vehicles to others creates a distinct exposure. Ask an appropriately licensed insurance professional to confirm how each vehicle and rental activity must be insured.

What if I do not have loss runs yet?

Request carrier-issued loss runs from the current carrier or agent. A new venture should clearly state that it has no prior operating history and provide its management experience, vehicle plan, rental agreement, and safety controls instead of presenting missing history as zero losses.

Does an independent passenger-car rental fleet need DOT insurance filings?

Not simply because it rents passenger cars. DOT and FMCSA requirements depend on the actual operation, entity type, authority, vehicle type, cargo, and interstate activity. Confirm the classification with qualified legal, regulatory, and insurance professionals.

How long does a rental fleet quote take?

There is no universal timeline. It depends on the fleet, state, requested coverage, completeness of the submission, loss history, and available carrier appetite. Starting 60 to 90 days before renewal gives a complex account more room for questions and corrections.

Does applying with Indem guarantee a quote or lower premium?

No. Applying does not guarantee acceptance, a quote, placement, binding, or savings. Insurance recommendations, negotiations, placement, and binding are handled by appropriately licensed insurance professionals and remain subject to underwriting and availability.
